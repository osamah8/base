# GOV.UK writing guidelines: working summary

- **Source:** [Writing to GOV.UK standards](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/), which replaced the old "Content design: planning, writing and managing content" and "Style guide" pages on GOV.UK (those now redirect here)
- **Read on:** 14 November 2026
- **Full text:** [GOV.UK writing guidance - full text/](GOV.UK%20writing%20guidance%20-%20full%20text/)
- **Status:** an agent's summary. The rules that bind this repo's output are in the root `README.md`.

## Tone

Specific, informative, clear and concise. Brisk but not terse. Serious but not pompous. Human, not a faceless machine. Avoid adjectives that make text sound emotive or like spin. Write as if talking to the reader one to one.

## Clear language

- Plain English, including for specialist readers: research cited in the guidance shows experts prefer it too.
- Short words over formal ones: "buy" not "purchase", "help" not "assist", "about" not "approximately".
- Explain a specialist term the first time it is used; after that it is not jargon.
- Active voice. Passive is acceptable when the outcome matters more than who did it.
- Split sentences over 25 words. No more than 5 sentences in a paragraph.
- Contractions such as "you'll" are fine. Avoid negative ones ("can't", "don't") and "should've", "could've", "would've", "they've".
- Avoid the "words to avoid" list, for example: collaborate (use "work with"), deliver (use "make", "create", "provide"), facilitate, empower, deploy, dialogue, and similar buzzwords. Break the term into what was actually done.

## Structure

- Front-load: most important information first, then taper to detail.
- Headings are descriptive, front-loaded and not questions. Avoid generic ones like "Introduction".
- Do not repeat yourself. No footnotes.

## Style conventions (from the A to Z)

- **Capitals:** sentence case everywhere, including titles. Capitals only for proper nouns and specific job titles ("Minister for Housing"); generic job titles are lower case ("director"). No block capitals.
- **Dates:** "4 June 2017". No ordinals ("2 June", not "2nd June"), no comma before the year. Ranges use "to", not a dash: "2011 to 2012", "10 November to 21 December". Short months (Jan, Feb) only where space is tight.
- **Times:** "5:30pm", "midday", "10am to 11am".
- **Numbers:** numerals, including 2 to 9. Write "one" in words unless it is a step, date or list point. Spell out a number that starts a sentence. Comma in numbers over 999.
- **Bullets:** always a lead-in line; more than one bullet; lower case start; one sentence per bullet at most; no "and" or "or" after a bullet; no semicolons or final full stop.
- **Abbreviations:** no "eg", "ie" or "etc"; use "for example", "such as", "that is".
- **Italics:** none. Use single quotation marks for the name of a document or scheme.
- **Brackets:** round brackets; never "document(s)", use the plural.
- **Hyphens:** only where the meaning is unclear without one.

## Where a CV differs from a web page

The guidance is written for government web pages addressed to "you". A CV and an application are about me, so "address the user as you" does not apply, and first person ("I led") is right for statements. Everything else carries over.
